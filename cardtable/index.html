How to play

  • Drag cards and piles to move them around the table.
  • Click a card or pile for actions: flip, rotate, shuffle, deal, draw, delete.
  • Double-click a card to flip it; double-click a pile to draw into your hand.
  • Drop a card onto a pile to place it on top; onto another card to start a pile; drop a pile onto a pile to merge them.
  • Drag a card to the bottom bar to take it into your hand (hands are private). Drag the bar's top edge to resize your hand cards.
  • Click a card in your hand — or 🔍 on a face-up table card — to see it enlarged (a click or Esc closes it; Esc also dismisses the action buttons). On a touch screen, press and hold a face-up table card to read it; let go to close.
  • A big hand fans out so every card's corner shows. On touch screens, swipe your hand sideways to scroll it; drag a card upward out of it to play it.
  • Pieces, dice and boards (🎲 Games → Draughts, Backgammon, Dominoes, Veiled Ranks): drag a piece to move it. From a ×N stack a drag takes one (drop it on a matching stack, its supply or a bag to put it there); Shift+drag — on a touch screen, ✥ Move all — moves the whole stack. Drag out of a supply (∞) to take a new piece; Shift+drag (or ✥ Move) moves the supply itself. Drop a piece on a matching stack to add it, back on its supply to return it, onto a bag to put it in, onto a tile of the same size (or its pile) to pile them, onto the hand bar to hold it.
  • Double-click a piece to turn it over, a die to roll it together with the dice touching it, a supply or a bag to take one out. Click a piece for its buttons: 🎲 Roll, ⟲ ⟳, ✂ Take 1, ✋ Hand, 🔒 Lock, 👤 Claim, 🗑.
  • On a board, a piece snaps to the square, point, hex or space under it: while you drag, a dashed outline shows where it will land and a chip names the spot (e4, p6). Pieces sharing a spot fan out, the top one marked ×k; a tap or a drag there takes the top one you may move. Dice never snap.
  • A locked piece (🔒; boards are locked) or one another player owns (select it: its owner's coloured dot shows) stays put: dragging it pans the view. Keys, with a piece selected: R / Shift+R rotate, F flips, Space rolls, Delete removes, Esc deselects. On a touch screen, press and hold a piece to read its face.
  • Several at once: Shift+drag on the felt (or on a board) draws a box around pieces, Shift+click adds one or takes it out; on a touch screen tap ⬚ (bottom left), then draw the box with one finger and tap pieces in or out. Only what you may move is picked (a chip counts the rest). Drag any of them and they all go; their bar — one row at the top of the table while you pick (it gets out of the way of any piece that comes there), or in the toolbar's place when pieces lie there; a swipe or the mouse's wheel scrolls it when it is wider than the screen — acts on them all — ⟲ ⟳, Flip, 🎲 Roll, 🔒, 👜 Into bag, 🗑 (more than five ask first), and R, F, Space, Delete. Esc, a click on the felt, ✕ or ⬚ off clears them and brings the toolbar back.
  • What's in a stack or a crowded spot: tap a stack's ×N (on a touch screen, press and hold the stack), a spot's ×k, or ⋯ Inspect in the bar. Take k off the stack, Take all to carry the whole of it, or pick one piece of the spot: tap it for its bar, or drag it out of the list.
  • Some games have named seats (Light, Dark): tap a seat label to sit there or to stand up and watch. Watchers are listed top right (👀); with automatic seating a watcher taps Sit down.
  • Peek shows you a face-down card (or the top of a pile), or any piece hidden from you, privately — but everyone sees in the log that you peeked.
  • Private pieces: 🙈 Hide (or H) turns a piece's back to everyone but you — on a die it is a cup over it and the unlocked dice touching it: roll them and only you see the faces. Yours show face up with 🙈 and a hatched corner; nobody else may turn, roll or take them; Reveal shows them to all. A rack or screen (hatched, "🔒 Alice's rack") hides whatever is set down in it for its owner — never shows anyone what they couldn't see; when the rules refuse a move of your own hidden piece, only you hear it; a bag's 🔒 Draw to my rack draws straight into yours. A tile taken off a rack is played face up; a unit leaving a screen stays hidden. Two games are made of it (🎲 Games): Dominoes (Draw 7 into your rack, then play a tile on your turn or draw until you can) and Veiled Ranks (hidden units: deploy behind your screen, ✓ Ready, then step and attack — ranks show only in combat; take the Banner).
  • Everyone gets a seat around the table, and the table auto-turns so your seat is at the bottom. Cards you play land facing you.
  • Scroll to zoom, drag empty felt to pan; on touch screens, pinch with two fingers to zoom, rotate and pan at once. Your view is yours alone — 🧭 resets it to your seat.
  • The host can enlarge the table (Table: small / medium / large) when more players join.
  • 🎲 Games: the host starts Hold'em, Cards Against Humanity, Bridge, chess, go, reversi and more in one click (or goes back to a free table).
  • Chess, Go and Reversi play by their rules: drag a piece — or a stone or disc from your own bowl or pile — and pick it up first to see where it may go (cyan). A move the rules refuse says why and goes back. Chess asks what a pawn on the last rank becomes; its buttons offer a draw or resign. Go has Pass, Resign, and Count once both players have passed (capture the dead stones first: there is no marking them); tap a stone to see its liberties. Reversi passes for you when you have no move.
  • 📜 Rules: the host can write a script that enforces rules (turn order, legal moves) and automates play (auto-dealing, a poker dealer). Everyone can read the active script; everything it does appears in the log as "📜 Rules". It runs beside the host's page, never on it: a script that hangs is stopped after a few seconds and the table goes on without rules.
  • The rules may ask you something: the question shows above your hand (❓) — tap an option, one of the highlighted pieces or spots, the cards in your hand it wants, or set a number; ▾ folds it away until you're ready. They may highlight where a piece you tap can go (cyan), and tell you something meant for you alone (🔒 For you, by the rules buttons). A strict game (📜 says so) allows only the moves its rules know.
  • Drag from your hand to the table to play it, or onto a pile to put it on top — toggle 🂠 face-down to play cards face down; ⇅ sort orders your hand by suit and rank (on your screen only).
  • Custom decks: upload one image per card — each card is named after its file ("dragon_rider.png" → "dragon rider"). Include a deck.json in the same upload for full control: 
    { "name": "My Game", "back": "back.png",
  "cards": [
    { "file": "dragon.png", "name": "Ancient Dragon", "count": 2,
      "meta": { "cost": 5, "power": 7 } },
    { "name": "Gold", "text": "Worth 1 coin", "color": "#eab308", "count": 20 }
  ] }
    Cards without a file render as text cards (no art needed). meta is free-form data that 📜 Rules scripts can read via t.card(ref). Extra images not listed in the manifest still join, filename-named.
  • 📦 Game boxes: a whole game in one upload — a game.json (its board, pieces, decks, setup and rules) picked with its images, or one .zip of them. Built in: Draughts, Backgammon, Dominoes, Veiled Ranks and Hexfront, a hex-map wargame skeleton (400 counters, dice, event cards and a turn script). Anyone may upload one; the host sets it up (that clears the table), resets it to its start, and exports the table as a box — the position, and with them the rules and their state — to keep it or go on in another room. A box's rules run only once the host agrees: a rules script sees every hand and hidden piece. A small one: 
    { "name": "Two Fleets", "players": [2, 2],
  "seats": [{ "id": "red", "name": "Red", "edge": "bottom" },
            { "id": "blue", "name": "Blue", "edge": "top" }],
  "pieces": [
    { "id": "sea", "kind": "board", "img": "sea.png", "w": 800, "h": 800,
      "grid": { "type": "square", "cols": 8, "rows": 8, "cell": 100 } },
    { "id": "ship", "kind": "token", "w": 60,
      "faces": [{ "color": "#dc2626" }, { "color": "#2563eb" }] } ],
  "setup": [
    { "piece": "sea", "at": "center" },
    { "forEachSeat": [{ "piece": "ship", "at": "seat", "owner": "seat" }] } ] }
    Every field — decks, dice, hidden pieces, hands, rules — is in the README's Game boxes.
  • Copy invite and send the link to friends — or tap QR so they can scan it with a phone. Everyone sees the table live over WebRTC (no server).
  • The game lives in the host's browser tab — if the host closes it, reopening the same link on the host's browser resumes the game.
